How it works

Every X-ray goes through the same five steps. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is the core: it compresses 16,384 pixels into a handful of "pattern strengths" that the classifiers — and you — can reason about.

  1. 1

    Standardise

    Convert to grayscale, even out contrast (histogram equalisation), resize to 128×128.

  2. 2

    Compress with PCA

    Describe the image as a weighted mix of a few "eigen-X-rays" learned from training data.

  3. 3

    Route

    A router model decides chest or bone. Images PCA can't explain well are rejected as "not an X-ray".

  4. 4

    Classify

    A body-part-specific model with its own PCA space predicts normal vs. abnormal.

  5. 5

    Explain

    Exact per-component contributions show what drove the call; a small decision tree spells out a step-by-step rule path.

The models

Measured on images held out from training. Chest images come from the pediatric pneumonia dataset; bone images are a mixed-region fracture dataset (hands, wrists, legs, spine and more), after removing rotated copies and duplicates.
